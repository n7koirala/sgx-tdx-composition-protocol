\subsection{NGINX Workload Overhead}
\label{sec:eval-nginx}

Where the LLM-serving evaluation of \S\ref{sec:eval-llm} stresses the
compute path of the confidential VM, the second application-level
benchmark targets the opposite regime: a syscall- and TCP-stack-bound
workload where any guest-kernel-boundary tax imposed by TDX, by Vordr,
or by the combination should be most visible.

\paragraph{Workload and platform.}
We deploy a tuned NGINX~1.27 (alpine, configured with
\texttt{epoll}, \texttt{reuseport}, \texttt{sendfile}, no access logs,
\texttt{worker\_connections=16384}, \texttt{somaxconn=65535}) inside a
GCP confidential VM (\texttt{c3-standard-8}, Intel TDX, 8~vCPUs,
32~GB~RAM, Ubuntu 22.04). The server runs in a Docker container with
host networking, and exposes two static routes:
\texttt{/1kb} (1024-byte payload, exercises the syscall-bound regime
where \texttt{accept}/\texttt{epoll}/\texttt{sendfile} dominate) and
\texttt{/100kb} (102400-byte payload, exercises the bandwidth-bound
regime stressing the TCP transmit path). The load generator is
\texttt{wrk2}~\cite{wrk2}, the coordinated-omission-corrected variant
of \texttt{wrk} that schedules requests on a Poisson process at a
target rate independent of server-side latency.

To prevent wide-area network round-trip time from contaminating the
sub-millisecond effects we measure, we co-locate the load generator on
a non-CVM \texttt{c3-standard-8} VM in the \emph{same} cloud zone as
the target. Internal-IP RTT between the two is consistently
\textless~1~ms, compared to the $\approx$21~ms RTT we observed when
driving from a workstation outside the cloud~VPC. We additionally
verify that \texttt{wrk2} on the load generator retains $\geq$50\%
CPU headroom at the matrix RPS (so the generator is not itself the
bottleneck).

\paragraph{Conditions.}
We compare three target-VM configurations:
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{native}: an identical VM image without TDX. This isolates
  the \emph{cost of confidential computing} from the \emph{cost of
  Vordr}.
\item \emph{tdx-only}: TDX enabled, but no Vordr agent and no
  attestation traffic. This is our baseline confidential VM.
\item \emph{tdx-vordr}: full Vordr stack — the in-CVM attestation
  agent, plus a verifier on the load-generator firing one incremental
  attestation round every $T$ seconds.
\end{itemize}

For each payload, we sweep the matrix in
Table~\ref{tab:nginx-matrix}: the attestation epoch $T \in \{15, 30,
60, 300\}$~s, the IMA baseline log size (\emph{cold}, $\sim$5K entries
just-booted, vs.~\emph{warm}, $\sim$100K entries pre-burned), and an
update-interleaving condition (\emph{no-updates} vs.~\emph{with-updates},
the latter triggering an \texttt{apt install} at $t{+}120$~s and a
\texttt{pip install} at $t{+}300$~s of the measurement window to
inject realistic IMA-log churn). \emph{native} and \emph{tdx-only}
are epoch-insensitive and run only the (log-size $\times$ interleave)
matrix; \emph{tdx-vordr} runs the full $4 \times 2 \times 2$ matrix.
We collect $N{=}3$ repetitions per cell on the \emph{no-updates} interleave
(for error bars on the matched-delta figure) and $N{=}1$ on
\emph{with-updates} cells.

Each cell uses a fresh VM provisioned from the same image, runs a
30~s discarded warm-up phase, then a 300~s recorded measurement phase
at a fixed target RPS. Per cell we record \texttt{wrk2}~HdrHistogram
percentile latencies (p50, p90, p99, p99.9), achieved throughput,
error counts, per-attestation-round timings ($\Delta n$,
\texttt{t\_round\_ms}, \texttt{pcr\_match}), and a 5~s-interval
sampler trace of CPU, memory, and IMA log size on the target.

\paragraph{Choice of matrix RPS.}
The matrix RPS for each payload is fixed at 80\% of the native
saturation point, calibrated separately. For \texttt{/1kb}, we sweep
target rates from 1{,}000~req/s up to 200{,}000~req/s on a fresh
native VM and identify the highest rate where (i)~achieved throughput
remains within 1\% of target, (ii)~zero socket errors and zero
non-2xx responses, and (iii)~$p99 < 2 \times p50$. The native NGINX
configuration described above sustains 100{,}000~req/s in a flat
latency region, with a clear knee appearing at 150{,}000~req/s; we
therefore fix the \texttt{/1kb} matrix RPS at \textbf{80{,}000~req/s}.
On \texttt{/100kb}, the same procedure identifies a clean ceiling at
25{,}000~req/s (corresponding to $\approx$21~Gbps of HTTP body
throughput, near the c3-standard-8 default egress NIC limit), giving
a matrix RPS of \textbf{20{,}000~req/s}.

\paragraph{What we are measuring and why.}
Our central claim is that Vordr's incremental-attestation protocol
adds negligible runtime cost on top of the underlying TDX confidential
VM, even on workloads that should expose the syscall path. We isolate
Vordr's marginal cost by comparing the \emph{tdx-vordr} condition
directly to the \emph{tdx-only} condition for matched
(log-size, interleaving) cells: this fixes the TDX baseline and
reveals only the cost added by running the agent and serving rolling
attestation rounds. The matched-delta figure
(Figure~\ref{fig:nginx-matched-delta}) is the headline result.
The \emph{native} condition serves as a reference for the underlying
\emph{TDX tax} — the cost of running the workload inside a
confidential VM \emph{at all}, independent of Vordr.

\paragraph{Results.}

\textbf{Throughput.} Figure~\ref{fig:nginx-throughput} shows mean
achieved throughput across the three conditions for both payloads at
the matrix RPS. On \texttt{/1kb} at 80\,kreq/s, all three conditions
sustain the target rate within 0.1\%
(native: 79{,}957~req/s; tdx-only: 79{,}938; tdx-vordr: 79{,}943).
\emph{The TDX tax on throughput is statistically zero, and Vordr's
marginal contribution is in the noise.}

\textbf{Tail latency.} Figure~\ref{fig:nginx-latency-tail} reports
mean p50/p90/p99/p99.9 request latencies on \texttt{/1kb} at the
matrix RPS. p99 latency is 1.95\,ms (native), 2.00\,ms (tdx-only),
and 2.01\,ms (tdx-vordr) — a 2.6\% TDX tax over native and a 0.1\%
Vordr marginal cost over tdx-only. Even at the p99.9 percentile, the
gap between \emph{tdx-only} and \emph{tdx-vordr} stays below 1\%,
within the run-to-run variance of \texttt{wrk2} itself.

\textbf{Matched delta (the headline).} Figure~\ref{fig:nginx-matched-delta}
plots the matched delta
$\frac{\textit{tdx-vordr} - \textit{tdx-only}}{\textit{tdx-only}}$
as a percentage, faceted by (payload, log-size, interleaving). On
\texttt{/1kb}, Vordr's marginal cost is bounded by $+0.84\%$ on $p99$
latency and $+0.07\%$ on throughput across the four configurations.
Two cells show \emph{negative} matched-deltas (cold/with-updates is
$-1.50\%$ on $p99$ and $-0.07\%$ on throughput), indicating that
Vordr's effect is statistically indistinguishable from zero at the
noise floor of our measurement methodology.

\textbf{Robustness across attestation epoch.}
Figure~\ref{fig:nginx-epoch-sweep} sweeps the attestation epoch
$T \in \{15, 30, 60, 300\}$~s on \texttt{/1kb} cold/no-updates, plotting
$p99$~latency overhead vs.~tdx-only. The curve is essentially flat:
even at the most aggressive 15~s epoch — 20$\times$ more frequent than
the 300~s extreme — $p99$~overhead vs.~tdx-only stays below 1\%. This
matches the per-round cost we measure directly: on a cold IMA log,
each incremental round completes in $\sim$77~ms (mean p95 across all
12 cold tdx-vordr cells); on a warm log it completes in $\sim$77~ms
\emph{after the first round}, with the first round taking
$\sim$4.5~s to perform a full IMA replay over the
$\sim$100K-entry baseline.

\textbf{Round-time decomposition.}
Figure~\ref{fig:nginx-timeline} shows a representative per-round
timeline (epoch $T{=}30$~s, warm log, with-updates interleave). Round~0
takes 4.5~s — the cost of replaying the entire pre-burned IMA log
once to establish the verifier's baseline state. Every subsequent
round is sub-100~ms, even with the periodic $\Delta n$ bursts induced
by the \texttt{apt}/\texttt{pip} update injection at $t{+}120$~s and
$t{+}300$~s. This validates the design assumption motivating the
incremental protocol: the cost of bootstrapping the verifier's view
of the IMA log is paid once per session, and amortised across all
subsequent rolling rounds.

\paragraph{Conclusions.}
Three findings emerge from the NGINX evaluation:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Vordr's marginal overhead on a syscall-bound workload is
statistically indistinguishable from zero.} On the matched
(tdx-vordr~$-$~tdx-only) delta — the cleanest measurement of Vordr's
own cost — $p99$~latency stays within $\pm 1.5\%$ across all four
configurations on \texttt{/1kb}. This is comparable to the run-to-run
variance of \texttt{wrk2} itself, and \emph{substantially smaller than
the 2.6\% TDX-over-native tax} that the same measurement isolates.
\item \emph{Tail latency is robust across the attestation epoch.}
Reducing $T$ from 300~s to 15~s — a $20\times$ increase in attestation
rate — does not measurably affect p99 latency, because each
incremental round costs at most a few milliseconds and runs only every
$T$~s.
\item \emph{The incremental-attestation protocol delivers its design
goal of cheap rolling verification.} The 4.5~s cost of the initial
warm-log replay is paid once per session; every subsequent round
runs in \textless~100~ms, regardless of $\Delta n$ bursts induced by
realistic update activity.
\end{enumerate}

Together with the LLM-serving evaluation (\S\ref{sec:eval-llm}),
these results demonstrate that Vordr's runtime overhead is
\emph{essentially free} in both the compute-bound and syscall-bound
regimes — the two ends of the application-workload spectrum that we
expect a general-purpose confidential-VM attestation system to be
deployed across.
