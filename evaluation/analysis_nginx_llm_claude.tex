\section{Evaluating Vordr on Different Workloads}
\label{sec:eval-workloads}

We evaluate Vordr on two end-to-end workloads chosen to bracket the
cost spectrum a confidential VM faces: an LLM-serving deployment
(compute-bound, multi-second per-request latency) and a tuned NGINX
static-file server (syscall-bound, sub-millisecond per-request). For
each workload we compare three target-VM configurations -- \emph{Native}
(no TDX), \emph{TDX} (TDX on, no Vordr), and \emph{TDX+Vordr} (full
Vordr stack with attestation rounds every $T$~seconds) -- on the same
\texttt{c3-standard-8} hardware on Google Cloud. The matched delta
between \emph{TDX+Vordr} and \emph{TDX}, holding the IMA baseline
log-size, update pattern, and model fixed, is our headline measurement
of Vordr's marginal cost; the Native--TDX delta separately isolates
the underlying TDX confidentiality tax. For each workload we sweep
the attestation epoch $T \in \{15, 30, 60, 300\}$~s, an IMA log-size
axis (cold $\sim$5K vs.\ warm $\sim$100K entries), and a workload-pattern
axis (no-updates vs.\ with-updates -- an \texttt{apt install} at
$t{+}120$~s and a \texttt{pip install} at $t{+}300$~s). Detailed
methodology (calibration, hardware, correctness validation, cell counts)
is in Appendix~\ref{app:methodology}.

\subsection{LLM Serving}
\label{sec:eval-llm}

We deploy \texttt{vLLM}~\cite{vllm} on CPU, serving Phi-3-mini and
Llama-3.1-8B (AWQ-INT4) at calibrated arrival rates of $0.15$ and
$0.03$~req/s respectively. Per-request latency is dominated by
multi-second matrix-multiplication time, so the dominant question is
whether confidential execution changes \emph{throughput}.

Figure~\ref{fig:llm-throughput-overhead} compares request and
output-token throughput across the three conditions at the
representative slice ($T{=}30$~s, warm IMA baseline, no-updates and
with-updates). On Phi-3-mini, moving from \emph{Native} to \emph{TDX}
costs $9.27\%$ on request throughput and $8.74\%$ on output-token
throughput; the further \emph{TDX$\rightarrow$TDX+Vordr} step adds at
most $2.12\%$ in no-updates and $0.42\%$ in with-updates. Llama-3.1,
evaluated farther from saturation, shows no measurable throughput loss
across any condition (matched-deltas $\le 0.053\%$;
Appendix~\ref{app:llm-detail} explains why).
Figure~\ref{fig:llm-duty-cycle} reports the corresponding wall-clock
\emph{attestation duty cycle} -- the fraction of measurement-window
time the verifier spends inside Vordr rounds. Across both models and
all four (cold/warm $\times$ no-updates/with-updates) cells at each epoch,
duty cycle stays in the low single-digit range ($1.07\%$--$3.21\%$)
and declines monotonically as $T$ grows. Even at the most aggressive
$T{=}15$~s, the CVM spends only $\sim$$2\%$ of wall-clock time in
attestation -- matching the throughput-overhead budget the matched-delta
measurement sees Vordr consume.

\subsection{NGINX Static-File Serving}
\label{sec:eval-nginx}

We deploy NGINX~1.27 on the target VM and serve two static payloads:
a 1024-byte response (\texttt{/1kb}) chosen to exercise the
\emph{syscall-bound} regime where
\texttt{accept}/\texttt{epoll}/\texttt{sendfile} dominate request
service, and a 102{,}400-byte response (\texttt{/100kb}) chosen to
exercise the \emph{bandwidth-bound} regime that stresses the TCP
transmit path and the cross-boundary memory transfers TDX
intercepts. The load generator is \texttt{wrk2}~\cite{wrk2}, run at
$80{,}000$~req/s on \texttt{/1kb} and $20{,}000$~req/s on
\texttt{/100kb} -- in both cases $80\%$ of native saturation.

Figure~\ref{fig:nginx-throughput} reports mean achieved request
throughput across the three conditions on both payloads. All three
conditions sustain the offered load to within $0.07\%$ of target on
either payload, with zero socket errors or non-2xx responses across
the entire campaign of 81 cells -- throughput is set by the load
generator, not by the server, in this operating regime.
Figure~\ref{fig:nginx-latency-tail} reveals the regime-dependent
behaviour. On \texttt{/1kb}, the latency distribution is tightly
compressed: at p99, \emph{Native} measures $1.95$~ms, \emph{TDX}
$2.01$~ms ($+2.7\%$), and \emph{TDX+Vordr} $2.01$~ms (a further
$+0.05\%$). On \texttt{/100kb}, however, the
\emph{Native$\rightarrow$TDX} step grows substantially: p99 latency
rises from $3.12$~ms (Native) to $3.38$~ms (TDX, $+8.1\%$ on the
mean and $+17$--$25\%$ on individual matched cells, see
Appendix~\ref{app:nginx-detail}), reflecting the per-page transmit
cost TDX imposes on large payloads. Critically, the
\emph{TDX$\rightarrow$TDX+Vordr} step does \emph{not} grow with the
payload: TDX+Vordr p99 on \texttt{/100kb} is $2.87$~ms, fractionally
\emph{below} TDX-only at p99, and the four matched-cell deltas are
within the run-to-run measurement noise of $\pm 5\%$ on this noisier
tail. Across both payloads, TDX+Vordr's matched-delta vs TDX-only is
small, and -- in several configurations -- statistically
indistinguishable from zero.

\paragraph{Cross-workload summary.}
Read together, the LLM and NGINX evaluations bracket the regimes
Vordr might be deployed in and reveal a sharp \emph{regime-dependence}
of the underlying TDX confidentiality tax. On
syscall-bound workloads, that tax is small (\textless$3\%$ p99 on
NGINX/\texttt{/1kb}, \textless$2\%$ matched throughput on Phi-3-mini
in the with-updates pattern); on bandwidth-bound workloads it is
substantial (\textgreater$8\%$ mean p99 and $17$--$25\%$ on
individual matched cells of NGINX/\texttt{/100kb}); on
throughput-sensitive operating points it is sizeable
($9\%$ on Phi-3-mini no-updates). \emph{Across all three regimes,
the marginal cost of enabling Vordr on top of TDX remains small
} -- bounded by $2.12\%$ on LLM throughput, within $\pm 1.5\%$
on NGINX/\texttt{/1kb} p99, and within measurement noise on
NGINX/\texttt{/100kb} p99. Combined with a wall-clock attestation
duty cycle that stays in the low single-digit range
($1.07\%$--$3.21\%$) even at our most aggressive epoch
$T{=}15$~s, this supports the central claim that Vordr delivers
cheap rolling attestation \emph{independently of the workload regime
on which TDX itself is more or less expensive} -- which is the
property a general-purpose CVM attestation system must hold.

\section{Detailed Experimental Methodology}
\label{app:methodology}

\paragraph{Hardware and provisioning.}
All target VMs are \texttt{c3-standard-8} instances on Google Cloud
(8~vCPU, 32~GB~RAM, Ubuntu~22.04). \emph{TDX} cells use Intel TDX on
the same machine type with Confidential Computing mode enabled. Each
cell provisions a \emph{fresh} VM from a common GCE image to ensure
that no state leaks between cells. Both workloads use a load-generator
VM co-located in the same cloud zone as the target so that internal-IP
RTT is sub-millisecond and wide-area latency does not contaminate the
sub-millisecond effects we measure.

\paragraph{Sweep dimensions and cell counts.}
For each workload, the matrix is the cross-product of three axes
\emph{plus} repetitions:
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Attestation epoch} $T \in \{15, 30, 60, 300\}$~s
  (TDX+Vordr only).
\item \emph{IMA baseline log size}: \emph{cold} ($\sim$5K entries,
  just-booted) or \emph{warm} ($\sim$100K entries, pre-burned via
  \texttt{generate\_ima\_baseline.py}).
\item \emph{Workload pattern}: \emph{no-updates} or
  \emph{with-updates}; the latter triggers an \texttt{apt install} at
  $t{+}120$~s and a \texttt{pip install} at $t{+}300$~s during the
  measurement window, injecting realistic IMA log churn.
\end{itemize}
\emph{Native} and \emph{TDX} are epoch-insensitive and run only the
log-size$\times$workload-pattern matrix. We collect $N{=}3$
repetitions per cell on \emph{no-updates} (for error bars on the
matched-delta figures) and $N{=}1$ on \emph{with-updates} cells. Per
payload, the matrix is therefore $4{+}4{+}32 = 40$ \emph{distinct} cells
expanded to $48$ runs after repetitions.

\paragraph{Calibration methodology.}
For each workload, we calibrate the per-cell offered load to $80\%$
of native saturation. Saturation is identified as the highest target
rate for which (i)~achieved throughput stays within $1\%$ of target,
(ii)~there are zero socket errors and zero non-2xx responses, and
(iii)~$p99 < 2 \times p50$. For LLM, we used vLLM's
\texttt{benchmark\_serving.py} on a fresh native VM, sweeping
arrival rates to identify per-model saturation; the resulting matrix
RPS is $0.15$~req/s for Phi-3-mini and $0.03$~req/s for Llama-3.1.
For NGINX/\texttt{/1kb}, we swept \texttt{wrk2} target rates from
$1{,}000$ to $200{,}000$~req/s on a fresh native VM and fixed the matrix
RPS at $80{,}000$~req/s ($80\%$ of the $\sim$$100{,}000$~req/s flat-region
top, with the latency knee appearing at $\sim$$150{,}000$~req/s). For
NGINX/\texttt{/100kb}, the same procedure identifies a clean ceiling
at $25{,}000$~req/s ($\sim$$21$~Gbps of HTTP body throughput), giving
a matrix RPS of $20{,}000$~req/s. We separately verify that the load
generator retains $\geq{}50\%$ CPU headroom at the matrix RPS so the
load generator itself is not a bottleneck.

\paragraph{Per-cell measurement and correctness.}
Each cell runs a discarded warm-up phase followed by a recorded
measurement window, during which we collect workload-specific service
metrics, per-attestation-round timings ($\Delta n$, end-to-end round
time, $\texttt{pcr\_match}$ verdict) from the verifier, and a
$5$-second-interval system sampler trace on the target (CPU, memory,
IMA log size). Across the entire campaign, every TDX+Vordr round
where new IMA entries arrived ($\Delta n > 0$) verified cleanly
($\texttt{pcr\_match}{=}\textit{True}$,
$\texttt{runtime\_verdict}{=}\texttt{CLEAN}$), confirming end-to-end
correctness of the protocol on real workloads.

\section{LLM Workload Details}
\label{app:llm-detail}

\paragraph{Models and inference stack.}
Phi-3-mini-4k-instruct ($\sim$3.8B parameters) and
Meta-Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct (AWQ-quantised to 4 bits to fit in the
$32$~GB host envelope) are served via \texttt{vLLM}'s
OpenAI-compatible API server with default scheduling and a $4{,}096$
max model length. The load generator is the upstream
\texttt{vllm/benchmarks/benchmark\_serving.py} driver replaying
ShareGPT-V3 prompts with open-loop Poisson arrivals at the matrix RPS.

\paragraph{Why Llama-3.1 is throughput-flat.}
The Llama-3.1 matrix is driven at only $0.03$~req/s, whereas the
Phi-3-mini matrix runs at $0.15$~req/s. At Llama-3.1's operating
point, the offered load is much farther from the native saturation
rate, so completed throughput is pinned by the offered load itself
rather than by the server's effective service rate. TDX and TDX+Vordr
do produce small per-request latency shifts on Llama-3.1, but they do
not trigger queueing-driven throughput loss at this load. Phi-3-mini,
by contrast, runs at a more throughput-sensitive regime where the
added latency of TDX is large enough to reduce completed throughput.
This is therefore an artifact of operating-point choice, not evidence
that Llama-3.1 escapes the underlying isolation overhead.

\paragraph{Duty-cycle composition.}
Duty cycle for a TDX+Vordr cell is $\sum t_{\texttt{round}} / T_{\text{cell}}$
expressed as a percentage, where $t_{\texttt{round}}$ is the end-to-end
time of each attestation round recorded in \texttt{attest.csv}. The
mean duty cycle in Figure~\ref{fig:llm-duty-cycle} averages this
quantity across the four (cold/warm $\times$ no-updates/with-updates) cells
at each epoch. In warm cells, round 0 alone -- the one-time full IMA
replay over the $\sim$100K-entry pre-burned baseline -- accounts for
$61$--$96\%$ of total attestation time depending on the epoch, which
is why duty cycle does not collapse to zero even at $T{=}300$~s.
The Llama-3.1 point at $T{=}30$~s rises to $3.21\%$ rather than
following the smoother monotonic decline; the underlying breakdown
shows two cold runs each contained one round with $\Delta n{=}0$ and
$\texttt{runtime\_verdict}{=}\texttt{CLEAN\_NO\_DELTA}$ but a connection-
or response-bound round time of $\sim$$19$~s, an artefact rather than
a sign of heavier IMA processing at that epoch.

\section{NGINX Workload Details}
\label{app:nginx-detail}

\paragraph{Server configuration.}
NGINX~1.27 (alpine) is deployed inside the target VM via Docker with
host networking. The configuration enables \texttt{epoll},
\texttt{reuseport}, \texttt{sendfile}, and \texttt{tcp\_nopush}, sets
\texttt{worker\_connections=16384} and a $65{,}535$ backlog, and
disables access logging to remove a non-attestation-related I/O
contribution from the measurement. Sysctl tuning on the target raises
\texttt{somaxconn}, the ephemeral-port range, and
\texttt{tcp\_max\_syn\_backlog} to values consistent with the matrix
RPS. Two static routes are exposed: \texttt{/1kb} (1024 bytes,
syscall-bound regime) and \texttt{/100kb} (102{,}400 bytes,
bandwidth-bound regime).

\paragraph{Load generator and coordinated-omission correction.}
The load generator is \texttt{wrk2}~\cite{wrk2}, the
coordinated-omission-corrected variant of \texttt{wrk}, configured
with $4$ threads and $200$ persistent connections per cell. The
two-phase wrapper runs a $30$-second discarded warm-up phase followed
by a $300$-second recorded phase with \texttt{--latency} for HdrHistogram
percentile output; warm-up data is excluded from all reported
percentiles. We verified that \texttt{wrk2} on the load generator
retains $\geq{}50\%$ CPU headroom at the matrix RPS so the load
generator does not become the bottleneck.

\paragraph{Matched-delta breakdown on \texttt{/1kb}.}
Table~\ref{tab:nginx-matched} reports the matched-delta
$(\textit{TDX+Vordr} - \textit{TDX})/\textit{TDX}$ per cell. Two of
four cells show \emph{negative} deltas on p99, and one on throughput,
indicating that Vordr's effect is at our measurement noise floor.

\paragraph{Bandwidth-bound \texttt{/100kb} results.}
The $102{,}400$-byte payload exercises the TCP transmit path and
forces every served response to traverse a sequence of large
cross-boundary memory transfers that TDX intercepts. At the matrix
RPS of $20{,}000$~req/s (\,$\approx$$16$~Gbps of body throughput),
all three conditions sustain the offered load to within $\le 0.07\%$
of target. Tail latency, however, behaves substantially differently
than on \texttt{/1kb}: the
\emph{Native$\rightarrow$TDX} step at p99 ranges from $+17.4\%$ to
$+25.4\%$ on the three no-updates cells where each measurement averages
$N{=}3$ repetitions (cold/no-updates, warm/no-updates, warm/with-updates) --
roughly an order of magnitude larger than the comparable
$+2$--$3\%$ TDX tax on \texttt{/1kb}. The bandwidth-bound regime is
where the underlying CVM cost actually appears.

The TDX+Vordr-vs-TDX matched delta on \texttt{/100kb} is small in
absolute terms and consistent with measurement noise on the noisier
\texttt{/100kb} tail
(p99.9 is itself in the $69$--$105$~ms range across conditions). In
the three no-updates cells with $N{=}3$ replicates, p99 matched-deltas
are $-1.31\%$ (cold/no-updates), $-15.48\%$ (warm/no-updates), and $0.00\%$
(warm/with-updates, $N{=}1$); the cold/with-updates cell shows a
$-47.5\%$ delta but is a single-rep measurement where both Native
and TDX-only also exhibit large p99 swings, indicating the cell is
not a stable matched-delta data point at $N{=}1$ repetition. The
qualitative finding is that TDX+Vordr is fractionally
\emph{faster} than or indistinguishable from TDX-only across the
three reliable cells, confirming that Vordr's marginal cost remains
small even when the underlying TDX cost is substantial.

\paragraph{Round-time pattern and the incremental protocol.}
The mean per-round wall-clock time across the $22$ TDX+Vordr cells
on \texttt{/1kb} is $77$~ms in cold cells and $4{,}538$~ms in warm
cells. The warm number is dominated by round~0, the one-time replay
of the pre-burned $\sim$100K-entry IMA baseline; subsequent rounds run
in $\sim$$50$--$100$~ms regardless of $\Delta n$ bursts produced by
the with-updates pattern. This validates the design assumption
motivating the incremental protocol -- the cost of bootstrapping the
verifier's view of the IMA log is paid \emph{once} per session, and
amortised across all subsequent rolling rounds.
